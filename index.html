Privacy Policy

My Trips · Last updated 6 November 2026

Short version: I don't collect anything. My Trips has no server, no account, and no analytics. I cannot see your trips. To show maps and forecasts, what you enter is sent to Apple and to any weather services you turn on; see Maps and Weather, below.

Where your data lives

Your trips are stored on your own device and synced through your own iCloud account, in Apple's private database. That database is controlled by your Apple Account. As the developer I have no access to it, and no way to read, export, or recover it.

Calendar, Reminders, and Notes

If you give My Trips permission, it references items you already keep in Calendar, Reminders, and Notes so that a trip can point at them. It stores a reference, not a copy sent anywhere. Nothing is moved out of those apps, and nothing is transmitted to me. Deleting a trip never deletes your calendar events, reminders, or notes. You can revoke these permissions at any time in Settings.

Location

When you add a place or a stop, My Trips asks for your location, if you have allowed it, to offer "Current location" and to centre the map. That position is sent to Apple Maps to find its name. If you add it to a trip, it is saved and treated like any other stop, including for maps and route weather. If you use the navigation button, Apple Maps starts directions from where you are. Your location is never sent to me.

Maps

Maps, place search, addresses and drive times come from Apple Maps. What you enter or add to a trip, such as place names and positions, is sent to Apple Maps to provide them. Some of this happens on its own, such as finding a missing address when you open a trip. Apple's privacy policy covers that. Nothing is ever sent to me.

Weather

When you open a route's weather, My Trips asks for forecasts for the places on that route: each stop, and points along the road between them. Each place is first rounded to a grid about 11 km (7 miles) across, so the exact address is never sent. No trip names, dates, or anything about you go with it — although, as with any internet request, the service can see your device's internet address.

Forecasts come from Apple Weather. If you turn on more weather sources in Settings › Advanced, the same rounded places are also sent to Open-Meteo and the US National Weather Service, and — only if you turn it on and add your own key — to OpenWeatherMap. Each of these services has its own privacy policy. Forecasts are kept in memory while the app is open, then discarded; they are never saved, and never sent to me.

An OpenWeatherMap key, if you add one, is kept in your iCloud Keychain, shared with the app FeelsLikeWeather so you only enter it once. It is sent only to OpenWeatherMap, with each forecast request.

On-device intelligence

My Trips can pick travel details out of what you give it — an email, a note, a screenshot, a photo or a PDF — using Apple's on-device model and on-device text recognition. That processing happens on your device. Nothing it reads is uploaded — not to me, not to Apple, not to anyone. A screenshot, photo or PDF is read and then discarded; it is not saved.

When you ask it to find the places an email or note names, to add them to a route, the names it finds are looked up in Apple Maps, as any map search in the app is. The email or note itself is not sent.

What I collect

Nothing. No analytics, no tracking, no advertising identifiers, no crash reporting, and no third-party code of any kind.

Children

My Trips is not directed at children, and collects no data from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it.
